# ryukoku-writing

A Claude Code plugin for Ryukoku University students who write reports and graduation theses in Japanese or English. One idea is at its core: **the claims are yours, the writing goes fast with the AI, verification is rigorous, the record is honest.** Because the AI checks your claims and theories hard, you can put your own theory or niche observation into words without worrying that a weak point will slip through. 日本語: [README.md](README.md)

> **This is not an official Ryukoku University tool.** The university's name is used only to describe who the plugin is for and which rules it follows (Ryukoku University, "Guidelines for students on the use of generative AI", 2025-11-14). No logo or emblem is used. The reading of the guideline is the plugin author's interpretation, not the university's official view. **The instructions of your university, faculty and instructor always take precedence**, in particular course-level instructions (guideline III-3: the syllabus or what is said in class). The guideline is revised over time (IV): check for updates with `python3 tools/rk/rk.py guideline-check`, and keep the plugin updated. Students of other universities should replace the rules with their own (`.ryukoku/config.yml` and the skills).

## Threat model (what this guards against, and what it does not)

The hooks deal with **an AI's mistakes, shortcuts and fabrication during normal use** (writing the manuscript when the course prohibits it, writing records or settings, adding unchecked sources or figures, posing as your confirmation). They are not a sandbox against a determined adversary, nor against **programs you approve**: an approved program can do anything you can.

There are two layers. (1) The hooks stop, or ask about, operations in forms they recognise. (2) What slips through is found afterwards: change detection during tool calls (msguard), `./rk check` and CI. Post-hoc detection is the backstop; you read the records and checks and decide. The guarantees below hold within this frame.

## How it works

- **The claims are yours.** Tell the AI your fragments, hunches and niche observations; it asks questions and organises them into claim cards (`claims/C001.md`). A card keeps your own words verbatim, apart from the AI's paraphrase, and every warrant or objection the AI adds is marked `[ai_suggested]`. Only you confirm "this is my claim", with a button on the evidence notebook (or `./rk confirm`). The AI does not invent your research question or claims and present them as yours (it does help you think about how to frame a question).
- **Rigorous verification.** The AI checks claim cards and manuscript paragraphs for logic (Toulmin elements, the four kinds of leap, fallacies, causal-inference errors), support (citations, their ledger state, whether a saved excerpt really supports the claim), counter-evidence (searched and logged), novelty (positioned against the recorded searches, never asserted) and **whether AI-written text swapped your claim for another**. Results go to `review/verification/` with severity (fatal / major / minor), evidence and suggested revisions. Without an excerpt the support is "unchecked"; without a search, "not searched".
- **Fast writing, when the course allows it.** The course AI policy (`ai_policy` in `.ryukoku/config.yml`) decides what the AI may do with the manuscript:

  | ai_policy | AI writes to the manuscript |
  |---|---|
  | `allowed` | yes; each paragraph's provenance is recorded automatically |
  | `limited` | asks before every write (check what the syllabus allows) |
  | `unknown` (default) | asks before every write (check the syllabus) |
  | `prohibited` | no; no claim cards or notation proposals either. Research, verification (without rewritten text) and format checks remain |

  Its only material is what you confirmed: claim cards, argument maps, ledger sources and saved data. Claims, figures or sources that are not in the material are not added to the text; they are listed outside it as "additional suggestions".
- **An honest record.** When the AI writes or revises the manuscript, the hooks record which paragraph of which section in `.ryukoku/provenance.jsonl` (append-only; each line carries the hash of the previous one). It is a record, not an alarm: `./rk facts` turns it into disclosure facts (share of AI-written paragraphs, breakdown per section, purposes). Your own edits are estimated from the differences between AI tool calls, and the record says they are estimates. Right after a write, a mechanical check runs (ledger state of the citations, uncited assertions, figures without a source, candidates that differ from the claim card) and the AI shows you the result.
- **Fabrication is blocked by structure.** No bibliographic data, statistics or pages from memory. Sources are checked by identifier (DOI → Crossref / DataCite, NCID → CiNii Books, CRID → CiNii Research, NDL ID → NDL Search) and kept in the ledger (`sources/register.csv`) as IDENTITY_CONFIRMED. **The exported bibliography is built from the ledger**: the metadata the identity check recorded from the registry (`sources/identity.json`: Crossref / DataCite title, authors, editors, year, container and more; CiNii / NDL the title) wins, and fields it did not record come from the ledger row. `sources/references.json` is never given to pandoc (`./rk check` only reports how it differs). Your VERIFIED covers the printed entry itself (registry metadata included), so a change to it removes the VERIFIED (a warning at export, an error at the submission check). One citation key names one ledger row. The export does not use pandoc's user data directory (templates in `~/.local/share/pandoc` and the like). A cited work (`[@key]`, `@{key}`, `nocite`; with `nocite: '@*'`, every IDENTITY_CONFIRMED ledger row) that is not in the ledger, is below IDENTITY_CONFIRMED, or whose row lacks a title and a name (author, editor or issuer) is an error at the submission check and the export (a warning while drafting). Keys are read by pandoc's rule (kanji or kana keys such as `[@山田2020]` included). Metadata that could replace the bibliography or its style (`references`, `bibliography`, `csl`, `citation-style`, `citation-abbreviations`) is an error wherever it appears, in the front matter or a YAML block in the body, and the export also overrides it with settings it builds itself; raw TeX, OpenXML or HTML that builds a bibliography (`\bibitem` and the like) is an error too. Author-year mentions and reference lists typed as plain prose cannot be checked. Figures are checked **per sentence**: a citation, a reference to a `data/raw|processed/` file **that no AI tool call wrote** (under `data/raw/`, one in the `rk.py data add` manifest with the same content), a **row of `data/numeric-claims.csv` that checks out** (its data file meets the same condition, its key names one row, the value matches), or your mark at the terminal that it needs no source (`./rk figures`; dropped when the sentence changes); otherwise a warning while drafting and an error at submission. `./rk check` reports rows of `numeric-claims.csv` that do not check out. Every number in body prose is a candidate (Arabic digits, and kanji numerals with a counter); exempt are years (with 年/年度/年代, in a date, a range of years, or English such as `in 2020`), dates, clock times, figure/table/equation/section numbers, versions (after v/ver/version or a product name such as Python, R, SPSS), standard identifiers (ISO and the like), ordinals (第1位, 2回目, 1人ずつ), counts of study-design elements (N 段階, N 群, N 問, N 項目, N 件法, 第 N 波) and numbers in headings and code. A four-digit number that is not in a year form counts ("有効回答数は 1523", "n = 2000"). Numbers spelled out in words ("three", 三つ, 半数) slip through.
- **Only two passphrase signatures.** VERIFIED (you read the original) and the final pre-submission confirmation (the content and the AI-use disclosure are correct). Every other decision (confirming a claim card, accepting a proposal, resolving a comment, approving a style rule, confirming you read a section, ...) is one button or one key, recorded with the hash of what you decided on.
- **Research leaves a record.** The search tools run only for a question id you wrote in `research/questions.md` and log every search (database, query, date, hit count, question, purpose), counter-evidence searches (`--purpose counter`) included.
- **Only an allow-list runs without asking.** Of the AI's shell commands, only (a) the plugin's own tools (while their content matches the shipped files) and (b) a fixed set of read-only commands run without a prompt. Anything else, and anything that cannot be analysed completely, asks you first and names the command.

Guideline III-4 warns against presenting AI-made work as your own. This plugin records what the AI wrote so that you can disclose it, and helps with the writing only when the course allows it. **Being able to record and disclose AI use is not the same as satisfying guideline III-4 (not submitting AI output as your own work).** Disclosing it, and following the course's rules, is up to you. Follow the course's instructions and ask your instructor how to disclose. This is the plugin author's reading, not the university's official view.

## First steps (one terminal session)

1. In Claude Code: `/plugin marketplace add …` and `/plugin install …` (above)
2. Open Claude Code in your thesis folder and run `/ryukoku-writing:start` (or say "start my thesis project"); answer the language questions and the AI initialises the folder
3. Open your own terminal in that folder and run **`./rk setup`** once (Windows cmd: `rk setup`; PowerShell: `.\rk.cmd setup`, since the default execution policy may refuse `.\rk.ps1`. **Setup and the notebook have not been tried on Windows yet**): the course AI policy with a one-line source (e.g. the syllabus section), the training opt-out check, the passphrase (now, or at your first VERIFIED), Git (if Git has no name or e-mail yet, it asks; they need not be real)
4. **Reopen Claude Code in that folder** (otherwise parts of the recording and detection do not apply)
5. Tell the AI your idea. Once it has made a claim card, run `./rk notebook` in your own terminal and open the URL with its key (`http://127.0.0.1:…/#k=…`) that it prints there. Check the card on the "Claim cards" tab and press "Record" if it is your claim. The key is printed only on that terminal; never paste it into the chat (the AI does not start the page, and without the key the page's API accepts nothing)
6. If the course allows it, ask the AI to draft or revise a section. It writes from confirmed cards, ledger sources and saved data only, and shows you the provenance and the mechanical check. You may of course write yourself
7. Ask for a verification ("verify this section against my claim cards") and fix fatal and major findings first. After reading an original, choose VERIFIED on the "Sources & claims" tab and sign with your passphrase
8. Before submission, on the "Before submission" tab (or `./rk signoff`), read the provenance and the disclosure statement, go through the checklist and sign the final confirmation with your passphrase

## About the passphrase

- It is needed only for VERIFIED and the final pre-submission confirmation. If you skipped it in `./rk setup`, `./rk verify` or `./rk signoff` registers it the first time.
- At least 10 characters, and either 3-4 unrelated words or 16+ characters (repeated characters, digits only or a common word are refused). The public key is in the project, so a short passphrase can be brute-forced offline. **Never type it into the chat, to the AI or into e-mail**; only into your terminal or the local evidence-notebook page.
- **Before typing it, check the "running code" location and version** shown in the terminal and next to the page's passphrase box. The human commands and the page check, before asking, that they run from the plugin location Claude Code recorded (the SessionStart hook records it) and that their files match the shipped MANIFEST; otherwise they stop without asking. This is a check made by the plugin's code while it runs: a program running with your permissions can rewrite both where `./rk` goes and the checking code itself (see "Does not guarantee").
- **If someone (or the chat) may know it**: register a new one with `./rk setup`. The new key is endorsed with the current passphrase, then the old key is revoked, and the revocation (signed with the old key) names the new key as its successor. An endorsement by a revoked key is invalid whatever its time, except for that successor. Signatures made before the revocation stay valid.
- **If you forgot it**: register a new one with `./rk setup`. The new key becomes this computer's trust root and the root change is recorded in `.ryukoku/root-changes.jsonl`; commit it on main and push from your own terminal (pre-push asks for `yes`). CI prints the root change and the new fingerprint first. **This relies on your advisor checking that fingerprint with you in person.**
- A message of the form "my passphrase is …", or a short one-line message that is your registered passphrase (also typed in full-width characters), is stopped before it is sent. Other forms may not be caught.
- On another computer run `./rk trust` and compare the fingerprints with the one you noted.
- Show the fingerprint (16 characters such as `8C77-CA09-…`) to your advisor. GitHub CI checks that main's history has a single root commit, takes the signers of the first `signers.json` in that history as its trust root and prints their fingerprint on the **first line** ("[trust root]"). **The advisor's procedure**: compare that first line of the PR's CI output with the noted fingerprint; if it differs, do not accept the PR and ask the student; if a "root changed" line appears, check the new fingerprint with the student in person. The same check works with HANDOFF.md for folder hand-overs.

## Commands

Human-only, in your own terminal: `./rk setup`, `./rk notebook`, `./rk confirm` (claim cards; a key press), `./rk figures` (mark figures that need no source; a key press), `./rk verify` (VERIFIED is a passphrase signature), `./rk signoff` (the final confirmation, signed), `./rk apply`, `./rk style`, `./rk resolve`, `./rk changes`, `./rk disclose`, `./rk optout`, `./rk trust`, `./rk import-docx`, `./rk update-tools`.

Anyone (the AI too): `./rk check` (same as CI; `--submit` before submission), `./rk facts` (facts for the AI-use disclosure, with the provenance), `python3 tools/rk/rk.py claims new|list|lint`, `python3 tools/rk/rk.py provenance materials|show`, `python3 tools/rk/rk.py verification scaffold|lint`, `python3 tools/rk/rk.py search …` (logged automatically), `python3 tools/rk/rk.py refcheck check`, `python3 tools/rk/rk.py export docx` (also LaTeX, PDF), `python3 tools/rk/rk.py bundle` (HANDOFF.md for an advisor without Git).

The skills are written in Japanese; when `feedback_lang: en` is set, they answer in English. Standing principles: [CONSTITUTION.md](CONSTITUTION.md) (Japanese, with an English digest).

Notation and format may be fixed directly by the AI when the course allows it (provenance records the purpose "format"). If you want to see each hunk, use proposal files (format-proposals): they can change only full/half-width forms of letters, digits, kana and a few marks, the punctuation system, spaces next to Japanese, heading numbers, diagram settings and the form of a citation of the same work; a proposal that changes any other character cannot be made (checked when made and again when applied).

## Settings

- `.ryukoku/config.yml` (the hooks act only where this file exists): manuscript language and feedback language (separate), course, AI policy from the syllabus (`ai_policy`) with its source (`ai_policy_source`), allowed uses in a limited course (`ai_allowed_uses`: research, verify, format, review, language, ideas, draft). No setting lets the AI invent your questions or claims and present them as yours.
- Plugin options (`/plugin`): `feedback_lang` (default for new projects), `crossref_mailto` (optional, empty by default, never embedded), `cinii_appid` (secret; never shown or logged).

## What this plugin guarantees, and what it does not

These guarantees hold within the threat model above: while the hooks run, for operations you have not approved.

**Stopped, or asked about, beforehand by the hooks**
- With `prohibited`, AI writes to the manuscript through Write, Edit, MCP and other tools are blocked (a symbolic link as the last component is followed; other tools' input is parsed as JSON, and every string inside it, arrays and nested objects included, except values directly under body keys such as `content`, `old_string`, `new_string`, `text`, `body`, is checked when it is shaped like a path (absolute paths even with spaces, `file://` URLs) or sits under a key that names a path; a relative path asks, and is blocked under `prohibited`). With `limited` and `unknown` they ask first. Manuscript writes are blocked when `.ryukoku/config.yml` is missing, unreadable or not UTF-8, has two `ai_policy` lines (indented ones count), its line is not of the form `ai_policy: allowed`, or **any detection of a change to a protected place (settings, records, ledger, tools) during an AI tool call is still undecided**. Only decisions you record at the terminal (`./rk changes`, `./rk sign change …`) or on the screen count; a line that appeared in a record file during an AI tool call is never taken as a decision. The hooks and `./rk check` use the same decision (`config_problems` in `rklib/common.py`).
- Shell writes (Bash, PowerShell) in a **recognised write form** (a redirection, a write command such as `tee`, `cp`, `mv`, `sed -i`, or a command's `-o` output) are judged on the path after lexing; a write that reaches the manuscript, `.ryukoku/`, the ledger, the tools, CI, the export settings, your questions or thinking log and the like is blocked under every policy. **The directory is never followed**: if a command contains any `cd`, `pushd` or `popd` (or a word that could become one after expansion: a variable, a matching glob), every relative write target in that command is unknown. An unknown target is blocked under `prohibited` or when the settings cannot be trusted, blocked when its file name or any directory name in it is a protected name (`main.md`, `config.yml`, `register.csv`, `identity.json`, `manuscript`, `thinking`, `.ryukoku`, `pandoc`, ...), and asks otherwise. Without a directory change, relative targets resolve against the tool's working directory. A newline after `&&`, `||` or `|` continues the list, as in bash. **Other commands** that can write (archive extraction, `patch`, `git apply`, `xargs`, `find -exec`, interpreters with `-c`/`-e`, subshells, a script you allowed) are outside the allow-list and ask first; their content is not judged beforehand.
- AI writes to your questions and thinking log, the human records, the provenance log, the ledger (`register.csv`, `claims.csv`, `identity.json`), the checking tools, CI and Git hooks (`.github/`, `.githooks/`), the export settings (`pandoc/`), Claude Code's settings, `~/.ryukoku-writing`, Git/Python configuration, shell startup files, directories on PATH, autostart entries, `.vscode` or `.envrc` are blocked, for every tool. CI, Git hooks and the export settings are yours to change, outside the AI; confirm a change with `./rk sign guarded tools --decision confirmed` (export stops until the export settings are confirmed; `pandoc/reference.docx` is not compared).
- Connections from the AI's shell to any host that reaches this computer (`127.0.0.0/8` and `::1` in every spelling, `0.0.0.0`, localhost names, hosts-file names) are blocked (no DNS query is made). The evidence notebook's API refuses requests without the key made at each start (shown only on your terminal).
- Shell commands outside the allow-list (the plugin's own tools and a fixed set of read-only commands) ask first.

**Found afterwards (the backstop)**
- A manuscript change made while an AI tool runs (a shell detour included) is recorded as provenance on the AI's side. With overlapping calls, a change while any call is open is the AI's. The window store is updated under an exclusive lock; when the lock cannot be taken within 8 seconds, the call asks you (it fails closed) and the changes until the next window are recorded as "uncertain". A window snapshot left without its entry, and a call that got no PostToolUse by the end of the turn, make the changes "uncertain" too; they are counted with the AI's. AI-written paragraphs left in a course recorded as `prohibited` are a warning in `./rk check` and an error at the PR and submission stages.
- A protected place (the ledger `register.csv`, `claims.csv`, `identity.json` and `review/annotations.json` included) that changes anyway is detected, written to the AI-use log and a second note on this computer, and `./rk check` and CI fail, and manuscript writes are closed, until you decide (a rewrite by the plugin's own tools such as `rk.py ledger` or `refcheck` matches what those tools wrote and is not a detection). A line added during an AI tool call to the provenance log, the AI-use log or the comment log that the plugin's own code did not write is a detection. A light confirmation in `signoffs.jsonl` counts only when the plugin's student-only path wrote it or it was appended outside any AI tool call. Where this computer has no notes yet (CI, a new computer, or a new project whose decision record has never been written), the two cannot be told apart and every light confirmation counts; a light confirmation added during an AI tool call is still itself a new detection.
- The export and the submission check build the bibliography from the ledger and check the figures as described under "Fabrication is blocked by structure".
- VERIFIED and the final confirmation count only with an Ed25519 signature by a trusted signer's passphrase. GitHub CI trusts only the signers of the first `signers.json` in main's history (and recorded root changes) and keys endorsed from them.
- A proposal file cannot change any character except by the allowed transforms (tests test_proposals.AllowList, test_allowlist_trust.HeadingNumbers).

**Does not guarantee**
- What programs or commands you approve do. They can do anything you can (rebuild records, notes and baselines, use the plugin's own code so their lines look like the plugin's, read the page key, create a pseudo-terminal, fake the passphrase page, switch the hooks off). Traces remain, but it cannot be stopped; read what a prompt shows.
- **Provenance is only as strong as the hooks.** While the hooks are off, and for programs running outside them, the record is wrong or missing.
- **Attribution of your edits is an estimate.** A manuscript change outside an AI tool call is recorded as yours (a detached process's late write too); a paragraph you edited right after declining a call, within the same turn, is "uncertain".
- That you read before you confirm or sign. **Light confirmations** (claim cards and the like) are not protected by cryptography; they rest on the hooks, the in-call detection and the append-only checks.
- The human-only commands' demand for a terminal (`/dev/tty`) is a speed bump, not proof that a person is there (on Linux with Claude Code 2.1.318 the Bash tool's child processes cannot open `/dev/tty`; other versions and systems were not checked).
- A DNS name that resolves to this computer is not recognised as loopback (the notebook's API is protected by its key).
- The mechanical checks (swap candidates, uncited assertions, figures without a source) are word-list candidates, not verdicts.
- Circumvention through combinations of allow-listed commands, or through configuration the AI or you placed, may be missed.
- Detection of writing or question-delegation requests (the context injected before sending) is vocabulary-based and misses rephrasings.
- Anything while the hooks are off (`disableAllHooks: true`, or the plugin disabled or removed). `./rk check`, `./rk setup` and CI report the disabling settings.
- Comments created on the evidence-notebook page have no identity check; an advisor's comments enter only through `./rk import-docx`.
- Whether a GitHub `pull_request` workflow runs the definition as changed in the PR was not confirmed (**unverified**). When a PR changes `.github/`, do not trust that PR's CI result; read the diff. Branch protection on private repositories needs GitHub Pro or above (GitHub Docs, About protected branches, https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches).
- Live runs with Claude Code were few. The hook's denial of an AI Write in a `prohibited` course was observed; whether the model attempts the write at all depends on the model (with the same request it once declined without trying). SELF-ASSESSMENT.md §2.3.
- The quality of the AI's drafting and verification, and behaviour on Windows and macOS (not tested on real machines; see SELF-ASSESSMENT.md).

## Privacy

Hooks and checks make no network requests; only the searches, verification and guideline check you allow do. Prompt text is never logged (only categories); the provenance log holds paragraph hashes and line ranges, not the text. Search candidates are kept outside Git (`.ryukoku/cache/`) and those older than 24 hours are deleted at the next search, SessionStart or `./rk check` (J-STAGE terms art. 3(5)); they stay while the computer is not used.
